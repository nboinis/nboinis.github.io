Measuring organic micropollutants in the livers of apex species is a powerful biomonitoring tool, revealing environmental pollution and chemical exposure at higher trophic levels. This critical review surveys recent extraction approaches for liver tissue (ultrasound-assisted extraction, pressurised liquid extraction, Soxhlet and QuEChERS) and compares them in terms of time, robustness, cost and environmental impact.

### In plain words

The liver of an animal at the top of the food chain keeps a record of the pollution that animal has been exposed to, which makes it a useful sample for monitoring the environment. Before any instrument can measure those chemicals, they have to be extracted from the tissue. This review looks at the four extraction techniques used most often for liver and weighs how fast, reliable, affordable and environmentally friendly each one is.
